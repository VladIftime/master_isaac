ased on the paper, here is the reward calculation structure for both Alice and Bob in Markdown format.
1. Alice (The Goal Setter)

Alice's objective is to propose valid goals that are challenging enough that Bob fails to solve them. She is rewarded based on goal validity and the outcome of Bob's attempt.

    Base Reward for Validity:

        +1 Reward: Alice receives this immediately if she sets a valid goal (defined as moving objects without pushing them off the table).

0 Reward: If the goal is invalid (e.g., no objects moved), the episode ends, and she receives nothing.

Outcome Reward (Bob's Performance):

    +5 Reward: Alice receives this if Bob fails to solve the goal she proposed.

0 Reward: Alice receives nothing for this component if Bob successfully solves the goal.

Penalties:

    -3 Penalty: Applied if Alice sets a goal where objects are outside the "placement area" (a specific 3D volume visible to the camera), even if the goal is technically valid.

Summary Formula for Alice:
RAlice​=I(valid)×1+I(Bob fails)×5−I(out of zone)×3
2. Bob (The Goal Solver)

Bob's objective is to move objects to match the goal state g generated by Alice. He receives sparse, goal-conditioned rewards.

    Per-Object Reward:

        +1 Reward: Bob receives this immediately when a specific object is placed within the success threshold (distance and rotation) of its goal state.

-1 Penalty: If an object that was previously correctly placed is moved away from its goal state, Bob is penalized. This ensures the sum of per-object rewards is at most 1 per object during a turn.

Completion Reward:

    +5 Reward: Bob receives this large sparse reward only when all objects are simultaneously in their goal states.

Turn Termination:

    If Bob achieves the completion reward (+5), his turn ends immediately.

If Bob runs out of time without solving the goal, he receives 0 for the completion component (failure).

Summary Formula for Bob:
RBob​=obj∑​(placedobj​)+I(all solved)×5
