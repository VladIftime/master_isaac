\section*{Section 2: Background \& Related Work}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 6 — Related Work: Reward Shaping (p.6)}
\item PBRS is the theoretical foundation — Ng et al.\ proved you can add $\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$ to any reward without changing what the agent learns
\item Policy invariance: the optimal policy is unchanged — you can design rewards purely for learning speed
\item Episodic validity (Grze\'s 2017): $\gamma_{\text{shaping}}=1$ is safe in finite-horizon tasks — the shaping telescopes to $\Phi(s_T)-\Phi(s_0)$
\item Our exponential potentials $\Phi(s)=w e^{-k d^2}$ build on Devlin \& Kudenko's dynamic potential framework

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 7 — Related Work: Curriculum \& Self-Play (p.7)}
\item Forced curricula: Narvekar and Portelas surveys show curriculum helps when rewards are sparse or exploration is hard
\item Our PPO-Curriculum uses a 3-phase pos$\to$rot staging — position first, then progressively add rotation
\item Automatic curricula (self-play): ASP (Sukhbaatar, Plappert) trains Alice and Bob adversarially
\item Time-based Alice (Sukhbaatar 2018): $R_A \propto t_B - t_A$ creates a self-regulating curriculum
\item We test both outcome-based (ASP-dPose) and time-based (TASP-dPose) variants

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 8 — Related Work: Geometry \& Mechanics (p.8)}
\item Quasi-static pushing (Mason 1986): object motion set by the friction limit surface
\item Limit-surface normality (Goyal 1991): every push couples translation AND rotation
\item SE(2) Lie-group distance (Park 1995): fuses position and orientation error into a single geodesic metric with characteristic length $L$
\item Why this matters: limit-surface coupling explains why the combined pos+rot gate is the universal bottleneck across all our models

\section*{Section 3: Problem Definition \& MDP}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 10 — Planar-Pushing MDP (p.10)}
\item UR5e robot pushes a T-block on a 1.4m$\times$1.0m table. 28D obs, 4D object-relative macro-action (21 bins/dim = 194k discrete). Object-relative actions achieve $\sim$95\% contact rate.
\item Transition: limit-surface normality couples translation and rotation — every push does both.
\item Success: pos < 5 cm AND rot < 0.2 rad. Validation: 30 held-out scenes $\times$ 20 trials $\times$ up to 30 pushes/trial.

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 11 — What Does Success Look Like? (p.11)}
\item Two-panel T-block visual: position-only success (position aligned, rotation arbitrary) vs combined pos+rot success (both aligned)
\item Success gate: pos < 0.05 m AND rot < 0.2 rad must hold at trial termination
\item 10/30 scenes are pos-only (rotation ungated), 20/30 require both
\item Scene solved if $\ge$1 of 20 trials meets the gate

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 12 — Why the Fractional Reward Failed (p.12)}
\item The fractional formula gives $\sim$0 or negative reward for 99\% of pushes. A 1 cm improvement from 30 cm earns $-0.17$ — negative reward for genuine progress
\item The penalty terms dominate the fractional improvement term, making the net reward negative even for progress-producing pushes
\item PPO multiplies the policy gradient by the advantage sign. Negative advantage $\to$ gradient pushes policy AWAY from goal-directed behavior
\item Sparse bonuses (+5/+2) fire on only $\sim$1\% of pushes — too few to overcome the noise

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 13 — How PBRS Fixes the Reward (p.13)}
\item PBRS replaces the fractional formula with $\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$ — a state-only potential difference that is always correctly signed
\item The same push earns $+0.133$ with PBRS vs $-0.17$ with the old formula. The sign reversal is key — gradient now pushes TOWARD the goal
\item Four properties: bounded (no reward explosions), Markovian (GAE-compatible), correctly signed (every push provides useful signal), policy-invariant (optimal policy unchanged per Ng et al.)
\item PBRS provides a guaranteed signal floor on every push regardless of distance

\section*{Section 4: Implementation}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 15 — Baseline: Fractional Reward at Scale (p.15)}
\item The original fractional reward at 2,048 envs reaches 83.3\% for single-agent — the task IS solvable at scale
\item Same reward, same environment, same budget — ASP with Alice/Bob gets 0\%. The adversarial goal distribution prevents any learning
\item This is the cleanest 1-axis comparison in the thesis: only the training architecture changes. Self-play is the bottleneck
\item All subsequent models switch to PBRS — not because the original reward is broken, but because PBRS is principled, efficient (works at 528 envs), and provides correct per-step signal even under distribution shift

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 16 — All Models at a Glance (p.16)}
\item Five models: PPO-Baseline (original fractional reward, 2048 envs), PPO-PBRS (PBRS single-agent), PPO-Curriculum (staged pos$\to$rot), ASP-dPose (outcome-based self-play), TASP-dPose (time-based self-play)
\item All share PPO+LSTM backbone and object-relative push primitive
\item They differ in reward shape and training-goal generation

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 17 — PPO-PBRS: Recommended Baseline (p.17)}
\item Simplest possible architecture: single PPO agent with LSTM+MLP, PBRS reward, sparse completion bonuses
\item Three reasons it works: PBRS provides dense gradient at every push, fixed random goal distribution avoids adversarial shift, single network avoids multi-agent optimization instability
\item Converges within 1500 iterations. Stable value function bounded by exponential potentials

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 18 — PBRS vs Fractional: Reward Signal Analysis (p.18)}
\item Left: simulated 5-push episode comparing PBRS vs fractional formula per-push reward. PBRS gives correctly-signed positive reward on every push; fractional formula gives negative or near-zero reward
\item Right: actual TensorBoard data — PBRS at 528 envs converges to lower position error than the fractional formula at 2,048 envs despite 4$\times$ fewer parallel environments
\item Both achieve 80--83\% validation SR — PBRS does it with 4$\times$ fewer envs

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 19 — Why PBRS for ASP? Decoupling Advantage from Value (p.19)}
\item GAE normally sums TD errors $\delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$. Under ASP with sparse rewards, those errors are just V-noise
\item PBRS adds $\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$ to every step — this term is always correctly signed because $\Phi$ increases when object moves toward goal
\item The $\Phi$-term provides a floor of correctly-signed gradient signal even when Bob's $V(s)$ is completely wrong
\item This is why PBRS lifts ASP from 0\% to 16.7\% — Bob gets genuine learning signal despite the non-stationary objective
\item But it only partially rescues ASP because the $V$-component still contributes bias. Single-agent has no distribution shift $\to$ $V$ is accurate $\to$ both terms align $\to$ 80\%

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 20 — SE(2) Unified Distance (p.20)}
\item SE(2) unified distance replaces separate position and rotation errors with a single Riemannian metric on the planar rigid-body configuration space
\item $L$ converts radians to meters. For the T-block with $L=0.07$m, 0.5 rad of rotation contributes 3.5cm to $d_{\text{pose}}$
\item Single-potential PBRS eliminates competing gradient problem: separate pos/rot potentials pull in different directions; SE(2) potential provides unified gradient
\item Both ASP-dPose collapses just like separate-potential self-play — SE(2) doesn't rescue self-play, confirming the curriculum is the root cause

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 21 — PPO-Curriculum (p.21)}
\item P82 trigger fix: old gate used per-push mean PosErr < 0.08m × 50 iterations — unreachable by construction. Fix: episodic position-only SR $\ge$ 0.5 over 20 iterations
\item Corrected PPO-Curriculum achieves 76.7\% SR, 3.3pp below PPO-PBRS (n.s.)
\item Position precision improves from Phase~1 training (0.023m vs 0.032m); rotation slightly worse (0.663 vs 0.568 rad)

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 22 — ASP Loop Diagram (p.22)}
\item Alice: 5-push free-roaming phase, object-relative actions, no goal observation. Final object pose $g$ passed to goal validation
\item Goal validation: valid ($>0.10$m displacement, +1), shallow ($-$1), OOB/airborne ($-$3), too-easy ($-$3 reset)
\item Bob: starts from clean reset at $s_0$; 10 pushes with PBRS dense shaping + sparse bonuses (+5/+2). PPOABC + GoalEncoder $\phi{:}\mathbb{R}^{22}{\to}\mathbb{R}^{8}$ + LSTM
\item Non-stationary goal distribution: Alice learns continuously, Bob's training distribution shifts every iteration. PPO importance ratio computed from rollouts collected under a previous Alice — dominated by distribution drift, not Bob's weight updates
\item GoalEncoder bottleneck: 8D latent may lose fine translation/yaw signal
\item Vanishing BC gradient: ABC buffer fills with near-random trajectories when Bob rarely succeeds — BC signal becomes noise

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 23 — ASP-dPose vs TASP-dPose: Alice Reward (p.23)}
\item Two self-play variants differ only in Alice's reward: outcome-based (ASP-dPose) vs time-based (TASP-dPose), both with SE(2) $d_{\text{pose}}$ Bob reward on the T-block. Both share ABC ($\beta{=}0.5$)
\item Time-based Alice aligns with solvable goals — she gains when Bob takes more pushes than she did
\item Time-based reward outperforms outcome-based: TASP-dPose 16.7\% vs ASP-dPose 6.7\%
\item But even the best self-play variant is 4.8$\times$ below single-agent PPO-PBRS (80.0\%)
\item Why ABC provides negligible gradient: Bob's MultiCategorical policy (194K discrete actions) concentrates probability on goal-relevant pushes. Alice's unguided actions lie outside this concentration $\to$ $\pi/\pi_{\text{old}}$ ratio extreme $\to$ PPO clip activates $\to$ zero BC gradient
\item In Plappert 2021's block grasping, Alice trajectories are smooth and repeatable. In contact-rich planar pushing, every push couples translation and rotation, making Alice's trajectories poor, non-repeatable demonstrations

\section*{Section 5: Evaluation}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 25 — Validation Test Suite (p.25)}

\item Full suite: 30 held-out T-block scenes — rotation-dominant (R\_*), position-only, combined
\item Sky-blue = object start pose, orange = goal pose
\item Every model evaluated identically: 20 trials $\times$ up to 30 pushes per scene

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 26 — Why Isaac Lab? (p.26)}
\item Throughput: Isaac 172 push/s at 528 envs vs Gym single-agent 209 push/s at 32 cores — raw speed comparable
\item Isaac delivers 7,920 transitions per PPO update vs Gym 960 (8$\times$ larger). Large batches stabilize PPO+LSTM gradients for self-play
\item Self-play and single-agent run at identical per-iteration wall-clock ($\sim$0.022 it/s). The budget is fair
\item Takeaway: Isaac gives self-play its best shot — large batches, fair budget, realistic physics — and it still fails (6.7--16.7\% vs 80\%). The 4.8$\times$ gap is structural, not a compute artifact

\section*{Section 7: Discussion}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 38 — ASP Diagnostic: Alice \& Bob Training Dynamics (p.38)}
\item Panel 1: Alice GoalValidityRate bar chart — ASP-Baseline 89\% (iter 1,267), ASP-dPose 84\% (iter 7,631), TASP-dPose-BP 91\% (iter 2,631). Alice learns across all variants. Alice is NOT the problem.
\item Panel 2: Bob's training SR — per-axis vs combined gate. Under original fractional reward: Bob learns nothing (PositionSR 1.3\%, Combined 0.07\%). With PBRS: Bob learns position to 54\% and rotation to 45--46\% individually — but the combined gate caps at 8.5--10.0\%, 2.5$\times$ below the independence product (24--25\%)
\item Key insight: PBRS provides enough gradient for per-axis learning, but the adversarial goal distribution prevents simultaneous satisfaction of coupled pos+rot gate. This is a causal bottleneck, not a measurement artifact

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 39 — ASP: The Empirical Result (p.39)}
\item Across 5 ASP configurations, no variant exceeds 16.7\%; single-agent PPO-PBRS reaches 80.0\%
\item Time-based Alice (TASP-dPose) is the best ASP variant at 16.7\% — 2.5$\times$ better than outcome-based
\item Self-play and single-agent have identical per-iteration wall-clock ($\sim$0.022 it/s) — the 4.8$\times$ gap is structural, not a budget artifact
\item Pos+rot success remains near-zero for all ASP variants (0--10\%) — the combined gate is the unresolved bottleneck

\section*{Section 8: Conclusion \& Takeaways}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 41 — Conclusion: Environment Efficiency (RQ1) (p.41)}
\item RQ1: PPO-PBRS at 528 envs matches PPO-Baseline at 2,048 envs — both reach 80--83\% validation SR
\item PBRS reduces the parallel-environment requirement by 4$\times$ for this multi-objective planar pushing task
\item Correctly-signed, bounded, Markovian rewards on every push enable stable on-policy GAE at lower parallelism
\item The policy invariance theorem is supported empirically

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 42 — Conclusion: Curriculum \& Self-Play (RQ2) (p.42)}
\item PPO-Curriculum 76.7\% vs PPO-PBRS 80.0\% — 3.3pp gap not statistically significant (p>0.05)
\item Self-play at 6.7--16.7\% across five configurations: original fractional reward (0\%), outcome-based (6.7\%), time-based (16.7\%), symmetric Bob-penalty (0\%), gym-pusht (13.3\%)
\item The substitution finding: PBRS substitutes for an explicit curriculum at this batch size. Regime-dependent — at gym batch 960, curriculum helps (50\% vs 10\%)
\item Self-play and single-agent cost the same per iteration — the gap is structural, not a budget artifact

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 43 — Conclusion: Individual Skills Under Self-Play (RQ3) (p.43)}
\item Training data from TensorBoard: Bob PositionSR 54\%, RotationSR 45\%, Combined SR 10\%. Independence product: 24--25\%
\item Under original fractional reward: PositionSR 1.3\%, Combined 0.07\% — no learning at all
\item PBRS provides enough gradient for per-axis learning, but adversarial distribution prevents composing skills into joint achievement
\item This is the mechanism behind RQ2's result: ASP degrades performance because it teaches decomposable skills under a distribution that forbids their simultaneous application

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 44 — Computational Overhead (p.44)}
\item Controlled, same-machine measurement: self-play and single-agent run at identical iteration cost ($\sim$0.022 it/s) on the same 528-env GPU setup. The 2-agent machinery adds $\sim$no per-iteration wall-clock
\item I deliberately do NOT claim a 5--10$\times$ self-play overhead — the only honest number is $\sim$1.0$\times$ per iteration
\item Key distinction: self-play doesn't fail because it's computationally expensive per iteration. It fails because it doesn't learn

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 45 — Limitations \& Future Work (p.45)}
\item Four paired limitation$\to$future-work items: (1) Bob penalty collapse $\to$ zero-sum game theory as stabiliser, (2) combined gate bottleneck $\to$ relaxed success criteria, (3) single-seed validation $\to$ multi-seed re-evaluation, (4) regime-dependent RQ3 $\to$ systematic batch-size sweep
\item These are concrete because each limitation is an empirical finding; each future-work direction is a specific experiment
\item Take-home: PBRS single-agent at 80\% is the practical recommendation; remaining open questions are about when and why to add complexity

\section*{Appendix Slides}

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 48 — PBRS: The Shaping Theorem (p.48)}
\item The fundamental theorem: add $F = \gamma\cdot\Phi(s') - \Phi(s)$ without changing optimal policies
\item Our potential: sum of two exponentiated distance functions. Cosine c(s) respects S$^1$ topology — no discontinuity at $\pm\pi$
\item Episodic PBRS: $\gamma_{\text{shaping}}=1.0$ is valid — sum over an episode is bounded = $\Phi(s_T) - \Phi(s_0)$

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 49 — PBRS: Why It's the Right Choice (p.49)}
\item Four properties that make PBRS ideal for contact-rich pushing: Markovian, Bounded, Zero-sum cycles, No constant drain
\item The old fractional improvement formula violated all four

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 50 — PPO-PBRS Training Dynamics (p.50)}
\item Left: training success rate over $\sim$3000 iterations. PPO-PBRS improves from near-zero to $\sim$9.4\% (strict combined gate)
\item Right: value loss stays stable — no explosion. PBRS exponential potentials prevent reward spikes

\item Time-based Alice partially corrects the adversarial curriculum by rewarding solvable goals — gains 10pp over ASP-dPose

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 58 — Appendix H: P82 Trigger Bug (p.58)}
\item Original PPO-Curriculum trigger never activated because it gated on an unreachable metric
\item Broken trigger required per-push mean PosErr < 0.08m for ALL 50 consecutive iterations — structural floor well above 0.08m
\item Fix (P82): gate on episodic position-only SR $\ge$ 0.5 over a 20-iteration lookback. Curriculum now activates

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 59 — Appendix I: BobPenalty Fail-Fast Equilibrium (p.59)}
\item Model I adds Bob time penalty on ALL phase ends including catastrophes
\item Bob learns that failing on push 1 ($-$11.0 return) beats trying for 10 pushes and failing ($-$20.0). PPO pushes toward faster failure
\item Alice's reward collapses: $R_A = 0.5\cdot\max(0, 1-5) = 0$
\item Lesson: asymmetric reward structure (Alice incentivised, Bob unpenalised) is necessary. This is a reward design failure, not a refutation of Sukhbaatar or Letcher

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 60 — Appendix J: Confounded Ablation (p.60)}
\item Self-play variants differ from single-agent on $\ge$7 axes: agent count, goal distribution, GoalEncoder, ABC, historical pool, episode budget, PPO updates/iter
\item TASP-dPose vs ASP-dPose is only clean 1-axis comparison — Alice reward type alone. TASP-dPose gains 10pp
\item The 4.8$\times$ gap cannot be attributed to any single lever. We present ``self-play as implemented,'' not ``the curriculum mechanism in isolation''

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 61 — Appendix K: Training Budget (p.61)}
\item All PBRS variants used identical budgets (528 envs, 3,000 iters). PPO-Baseline used more compute for architectural comparison (2,048 envs)
\item PPO-Baseline received $\sim$129$\times$ more push-macros than PBRS models
\item Both converge to 80--83\% — PBRS achieves comparable performance with a fraction of the push budget
\item Max\_iter values are SLURM caps; actual training stopped earlier

\newpage
\slidesec{Slide 62 — Appendix L: Validation Protocol (p.62)}
\item All models evaluated under identical protocol: 30 T-block scenes, 20 trials per scene, up to 30 pushes/trial
\item Success gate: pos\_err < 0.05 m AND rot\_err < 0.2 rad at trial termination
\item Disclosed limitations: single-seed validation, best-of-20 inflates scene SR (PPO-PBRS trial SR 66\% $\to$ scene SR 80\%)
